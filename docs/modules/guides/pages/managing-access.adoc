= Managing access

This guide is for the administrator of a running Wagoe app: the person who
decides who may sign in and what they may do there. It covers the ideas, the
first admin account, and switching an app's checks on. The steps are in
xref:access-tasks.adoc[Access tasks]; when something does not work, see
xref:access-troubleshooting.adoc[Access troubleshooting].

Developers declaring permissions and route rules want
xref:authorization.adoc[Authorization] instead.

== The ideas

User:: Someone who can sign in. A user is active or deactivated.

Platform admin:: A user with the role *Admin* and no tenant. A platform admin
may do everything, whatever their groups. Users, groups and roles are managed
by users with the role Admin. Give it to as few people as you can.

Permission:: One thing a user may do, written `<module>/<action>`:
`orders/read`, `orders/write`, `orders/refund`. Each admin entity has two:
`admin.orders/read` opens the orders list, `admin.orders/write` changes
orders.

Role:: A named set of permissions, such as `orders/viewer`. There are two kinds:
+
* *Code roles* come with the app. They are locked; you cannot change them.
* *Managed roles* are the ones you make, from a checklist of permissions.

Group:: A set of users. You give rights to a group, never to one user. Members
are added by hand, or come from your identity provider when users sign in with
single sign-on (SSO); those memberships are read-only here.

Everyone:: A built-in group of every signed-in user, including people who
sign up later. Give it what every user should be able to do.

Assignment:: A role on a group, with an optional *scope*. The scope narrows
the role to part of the data, such as one domain or one customer. No scope
means everywhere. A scope value is fixed (`domain 4`), or taken from the user
(`the member's department`), so one group serves every department.

A user may do what the roles on all their groups allow, each within its scope.
Rights add up: nothing a group gives is taken away by another. A change counts
from the user's next click; they do not need to sign in again.

== A worked example

Sam answers support tickets for one customer. He should read that customer's
tickets and nothing else.

. Create a group *Support Customer 001* and add Sam.
. On the group, assign the role `admin.tickets/viewer` with the scope
  *customer: Customer 001*.
. Open Sam's *Rights* tab. It lists `admin.tickets/read`, from that role and
  group, with the customer as its scope.
. Sign in as Sam in a private window. The admin shows *Tickets* and nothing
  else, and the list holds only Customer 001's tickets. A ticket of another
  customer answers *not found*, and there is no button to change one.

To let Sam answer the tickets as well, assign `admin.tickets/editor` instead.
For a second customer, assign the role again with that customer: rights add
up. Each step is spelled out in xref:access-tasks.adoc[Access tasks].

== The first admin

A new app has no users. On the server, from the app's directory, create the
first platform admin:

[source,bash]
----
bb create-admin                  # asks for email, name and password
bb create-admin --env prod       # against the production configuration
----

The database must be migrated first: `clojure -M:migrate up`, unless the app
migrates when it starts. The password must meet the app's password policy and
may not contain the email address.

Then sign in at `/web/login`. You land on the dashboard, `/web/admin/`. The
sidebar's *Users* section holds everything this guide uses: *Users*, *Groups*,
*Roles*, *Permissions* and *Audit Trail*.

Under `:enforce`, every other new user signs in to an empty admin until a
group of theirs has a role.

== Switching checks on

An app's configuration (`config.edn`) sets how strictly rights are checked,
with `:wagoe/authorization {:mode ...}`:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| `:off` | Only sign-in is checked. The default for apps made before this release.
| `:report` | Nothing is refused, but each refusal `:enforce` would make is logged.
| `:enforce` | Refusals hold. The default for a new app.
|===

xref:upgrade-authorization.adoc[Upgrading an app to authorization] takes an
existing app through these steps with a guided command. By hand:

. *Upgrade and start once.* The first start copies the old rights over:
  Everyone gets every module's `editor` role, so nobody can do less than
  before, and users who had the role *Viewer* join a group *Readers*. Nobody
  becomes an admin.
. *Grant the admin.* A user with the role Admin who belongs to a tenant is
  not a platform admin. Under `:enforce` they see only the admin entities a
  group gives them: assign `admin.<entity>/viewer` or `/editor` on a group of
  theirs.
. *Switch to `:report`* and run the app as usual for a few days. Each refusal
  is logged as a warning, at most once a minute per user and route:
+
----
Authorization would refuse this request under :enforce {:user-id … :route "/api/v1/orders" :method :post :permission :orders/write :reason :forbidden}
Admin authorization would refuse this under :enforce {:user-id … :entity :orders :permission :admin.orders/write}
Admin authorization would keep this user out of the admin under :enforce {:user-id …}
----
+
For each line, decide whether that user should be allowed. If so, give one of
their groups a role holding the permission. If not, leave it: that is what
`:enforce` is for. A route logged with no permission has no rule at all; that
is for a developer, and `clojure -M:run authz-report` lists every such route.
. *Switch to `:enforce`* when the lines you meant to fix have stopped.
. *Narrow Everyone.* Replace the `editor` roles the upgrade gave it with what
  every user should really have, often the `viewer` roles.

To roll back, set the mode to `:report` or `:off` and restart. Groups, roles
and assignments stay as they are, ready for the next try.
