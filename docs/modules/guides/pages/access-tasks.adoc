= Access tasks

Each task below is done in the admin, signed in as a platform admin, and ends
with how to check it worked. The ideas behind them are in
xref:managing-access.adoc[Managing access].

== Users

=== Add a user

. Go to *Users* and choose *New User*.
. Fill in name, email and a first password. The form lists the password rules.
. Leave *Role* on *User*. *Admin* makes a platform admin.
. Untick *Send Welcome Email* if you will pass the password on yourself.
. Choose *Create User*.

The user can sign in now, with the rights of Everyone. Add them to a group for
more.

Check:: Open the user and their *Rights* tab: *Everyone* is listed.

=== Deactivate or reactivate a user

. Go to *Users* and open the user.
. Untick *Active* and choose *Update*.

Deactivating ends all their sessions at once. Sign-in then answers *Invalid
email or password*, so nothing tells an outsider the account exists. Tick
*Active* again to reactivate; group memberships are kept.

Check:: The *Sessions* tab shows *No active sessions*.

=== End a session

A user's *Sessions* tab lists each device signed in, with its IP address and
when it was last used. *Revoke* signs that device out. To sign someone out
everywhere, deactivate them.

=== Two-factor authentication

Users turn two-factor authentication (MFA) on and off themselves, under
*Profile & Security*. Turning it off asks for their password or a current
code; a backup code counts.

A user who has lost both the device and the backup codes cannot turn it off
themselves. Open their *Sessions* tab and choose *Reset two-factor
authentication* under *Sign-in*. They sign in with their password alone until
they set it up again. The reset is in the audit trail, with you as actor. Your
own account is not reset here: turn yours off under *Profile & Security*.

Check:: The *Sessions* tab says *Two-factor authentication is off*.

=== Locked accounts

Five wrong passwords in a row lock an account for 15 minutes. Wrong MFA codes
and a wrong current password on the change-password form count too. The user
sees *Too many failed attempts, please try again in 15 minutes*, with the
minutes still left; the API answers 429 with a `Retry-After` header.

The lock ends by itself. To end it sooner, open the user's *Sessions* tab and
choose *Unlock* under *Sign-in*; that is audited too. A successful sign-in
resets the count. A developer sets both numbers in `config.edn`:

[source,clojure]
----
:wagoe/user {:auth {:max-failed-attempts      5
                    :lockout-duration-minutes 15}}
----

=== Password policy

The rules a new password must meet are in `config.edn`, under
`:wagoe/settings :user-validation :password-policy`:

[source,clojure]
----
{:min-length             8      ; the defaults
 :max-length             255
 :require-uppercase?     false
 :require-lowercase?     false
 :require-numbers?       true
 :require-special-chars? false
 :forbidden-patterns     #{}}
----

The new-user form and the change-password form list the rules in force. They
apply to passwords set from then on; existing passwords keep working.

== Groups

=== Create a group

. Go to *Groups*.
. Under *New group*, give it a name and a short description of who belongs in
  it.
. Choose *Create group*. Its page opens.

=== Add or remove members

. On the group's page, type part of a name or email under *User to add*.
. Pick the user from the list and choose *Add member*.

*Remove* beside a member takes them out. Their rights from this group end with
their next click.

Check:: The member's *Rights* tab lists the group, with source *Added*.

Everyone has no member list: every signed-in user is in it.

=== Memberships from your identity provider

When users sign in with SSO, the app can map the groups your identity
provider (Entra ID, LDAP, …) puts them in to groups here, by name. A developer
sets this up once; see
xref:authentication.adoc#_memberships_from_an_identity_provider[Memberships from an identity provider].
Such memberships:

* are read-only here: change them at the provider;
* follow the provider, because they are not stored;
* count only when a group here has exactly that name.

On a group's page, *From the identity provider* and *Show them* list them. On a
user's *Rights* tab they carry the source *Identity provider*.

=== Delete a group

*Delete group* on its page. Its members lose what its roles gave them; the
users themselves stay. Everyone cannot be deleted.

== Roles

Go to *Roles*. The list shows each role, how many permissions it holds, and its
source: *Code* or *Managed*.

=== Code roles

Each module ships at least `<module>/viewer` (read) and `<module>/editor`
(read and change); scaffolded modules add `<module>/manager`. Each admin entity
has `admin.<entity>/viewer` and `admin.<entity>/editor`. A code role's page
lists its permissions, locked, and who holds it.

.A code role: locked, with Duplicate and who holds it
image::access/code-role.png[A code role's page]

=== Make a managed role

. Under *New role*, give it a name such as `support/agent`, and a description.
. Tick the permissions it holds. They are grouped by module.
. Choose *Create role*.

A role needs a name and at least one permission. To change it later, open it,
change the ticks and choose *Save*. *Delete role* removes it and its
assignments.

=== Adapt a code role

. Open the code role.
. Under *Duplicate*, name the copy and choose *Duplicate role*.
. Change the copy's ticks and choose *Save*.

The copy is a managed role; the code role is unchanged. Assign the copy
instead of the original.

== Assignments

=== Give a group a role

. Open the group.
. Under *Roles*, pick the role.
. For each scope key the app has, such as *Domain* or *Customer*, either leave
  it empty, which means everywhere, or pick a value.
. Choose *Assign role*.

.Assigning a role limited to one customer
image::access/assign-role.png[The role form with a customer picked]

To give the role in two domains, assign it twice. *Remove* beside an
assignment ends it.

=== Take the scope from the member

When the app knows something about each user, say their department, a scope
key can offer *Where Department comes from*. Choose *the member's department*
instead of *Fixed value*. Each member then holds the role in their own
department, and keeps holding it there when they move, without anyone editing
the group.

If the app finds no department for a member, that member gets nothing from
this assignment, not everything.

=== Give every user something

Open the *Everyone* group and assign the role there. A role on Everyone holds
for every signed-in user, today's and tomorrow's.

== Check what someone may do

After any change, check it one of these ways.

The user's Rights tab::
Open the user and choose *Rights*. *Groups* lists each group and why they are
in it: *Added*, *Everyone* or *Identity provider*. *Effective permissions*
lists every permission with the role, group and scope it comes from. A scope
taken from the member shows the value it resolved to and its source, or
*(none, so no rights)*. A platform admin's tab says they pass every check.
+
image::access/user-rights.png[A user's Rights tab]

Who holds a permission or role::
*Permissions* lists every permission the app has. Open one, or a role on the
*Roles* page, to see the roles, groups and users holding it. Members from an
identity provider are not in the users list; see the group's page.
+
image::access/permission-holders.png[Who holds a permission]

Sign in as them::
Make a test user, put them in the same groups, and sign in in a private
window. The admin's sidebar and dashboard show only what they may read.

The audit trail::
*Audit Trail* lists who added whom to which group, which roles were assigned,
sign-ins, deactivations and more, with the administrator who did it. Filter on
the action or result. Only platform admins see it.

The route report::
On the server, `clojure -M:run authz-report` prints every route with the
permission it needs, and the routes with no rule, which only a platform admin
passes. It does not start the app.
